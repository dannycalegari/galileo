=
BOOK ONE, //
ON WRITING. //
CHAPTER ONE. //
On the letters. //

The consonant letters of the Arabs are twenty-eight, whose names, order and shapes, with their sounds, the following table shows.

[The table: each letter's number, its name (Elif, Be, Te, The, Gjim...), its sound in Latin letters, and its four shapes, standing alone, joined to the letter before, joined to the letter after, and joined on both sides.]

page
Caeterum, cum literae Arabicae omnes, praeter sex, sequenti in eadem dictione inter scribendum annectantur, & in fine liberiori ductu protrahantur, ejusdem literae, ratione connexionis & situs, plures ortae sunt figurae.
=
Moreover, since all the Arabic letters but six are joined, in writing, to the letter that follows in the same word, and at the end of a word are drawn out with a freer stroke, the same letter has come to have several shapes, according to how it is joined and where it stands.

[You copy out the table, letter by letter and shape by shape, and then the words Erpenius gives to practise on, until the joined letters stop looking like a vine and start looking like words. You read Arabic now: slowly, with the book open beside you, but you read it.]
