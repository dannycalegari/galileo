page
Sir, //

I send you, as I promised at Eton, what I have seen with my trunks of glasses. The first of the Moon I drew in July of the year 1609, before ever I heard of the Italian's book; the whole face of her I drew this last winter, with the great spots in their places, and I think it truer than his.

But this is the matter I would have you keep close. On the 8th of December last, in the morning, the Sun being newly up and so dimmed with a mist that I might look on him through the glass, I saw in his face black spots. I have watched him since on many mornings. The spots go over him from the east side to the west in some fourteen days, and change their shapes, and new ones come; so that, as I judge, the Sun turns upon himself, as the Earth does.

I would not have this printed. My lord of Northumberland is in the Tower these six years, and I have been in prison once already for keeping his company; there are those who would call it atheism to say the Sun has spots. Let the Italians have the glory, and I the quiet. //

Your loving friend, //
Tho. Harriot. //
Syon, the 20th of April 1611.
