[A small worn quarto, chained like everything else in the room. Every English schoolboy learns from it, from the age of seven, by heart; this copy has been beaten into shape by a whole school before it came here.]

AN INTRODUCTION OF THE EIGHT PARTS OF LATIN SPEECH. //

In Speech be these eight Parts following: //
Noun, Pronoun, Verb, Participle, declined; //
Adverb, Conjunction, Preposition, Interjection, undeclined. //

OF A NOUN. //

A Noun is the name of a thing that is, and may be seen, felt, heard, or understood: as the name of my hand in Latin is manus; the name of an house is domus; the name of goodness is bonitas.

page
Of Nouns, some be Substantives, and some be Adjectives.

A Noun Substantive is that standeth by himself, and requireth not another word to be joined with him to shew his signification: as homo, a man.

A Noun Substantive either is Proper to the thing that it betokeneth, as Edwardus is my proper name; or else is Common to more, as homo is a common name to all men.

A Noun Adjective is that cannot stand by himself, but requireth to be joined with another word: as bonus, good; pulcher, fair.

PROPRIA QUAE MARIBUS. //

Propria quae maribus tribuuntur, mascula dicas: //
Ut sunt divorum; Mars, Bacchus, Apollo: virorum; //
Ut Cato, Virgilius: fluviorum; ut Tibris, Orontes: //
Mensium; ut October: ventorum; ut Libs, Notus, Auster. //

[The verses go on for pages: which nouns are masculine, which feminine, which neither, and every exception, to be chanted aloud. In the margin a boy has written "Thomas Kyte his book, 1601", and under it "and a vile book it is".]

CARMEN DE MORIBUS. //

Qui mihi discipulus, puer, es, cupis atque doceri, //
Huc ades, haec animo concipe dicta tuo. //
Mane citus lectum fuge, mollem discute somnum; //
Templa petas supplex, et venerare Deum. //

[You sit over the grammar through the afternoon, declining and conjugating under your breath, as English boys do for seven years. At the end you can make out a page of Latin, slowly, with your finger under the words.]
