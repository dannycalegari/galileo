# Coins. Every country pays in its own: what you are paid is in the coin of the place, and what
# you pay is taken from that coin first; anything more is paid in foreign coin, which is taken a
# tenth below its worth. A money-changer (role changer, in the market towns) changes your foreign
# coin for the coin of the place, for a fee of three in the hundred ({change}), or into another
# coin ({change ID}). Amounts are reckoned, as everywhere in the game, in the money of account of
# Paris (livres, sous and deniers): what a coin is worth is fixed in those, except where a coin is
# DEAR, which a changer there pays more for (the silver of the Indies, wanted in the Levant).
#
#   coin ID
#     name NAME               what you carry: "12 livres in French coin"
#     look TEXT               in the purse (I)
#     in PLACES               where it is the coin of the place: towns, or countries (the people's
#                             files' "country"; France where none is given); a town before its country
#     dear PLACES FACTOR      a changer in those places pays FACTOR times its worth (and asks it)
#   end
#   PLACES: names with commas between and _ for a space

coin french
  name French coin
  look The King's silver quarts d'écu, the douzains and liards of every day, and the gold écu au soleil for large sums, all reckoned in livres, sous and deniers.
  in France
end

coin english
  name English coin
  look King Charles's shillings and sixpences, and the gold unite and laurel: reckoned in pounds, shillings and pence, and heavier than a Frenchman expects.
  in England
end

coin dutch
  name Dutch guilders
  look Guilders, stuivers and lion dollars of the States General. The bank of Amsterdam's money, written in its books, is worth more than its own coin.
  in Netherlands
end

coin thaler
  name thalers
  look Thalers of the Emperor, of Saxony and of a dozen bishops, and their groschen and kreuzers: the coin of the Empire, which the war has clipped and debased until nobody quite trusts it.
  in Empire,Bohemia,Switzerland
end

coin venetian
  name Venetian ducats
  look Silver ducats and lire of the Most Serene Republic, and her gold zecchini, taken from London to Aleppo.
  in Venice,Padua
  dear Constantinople,Ragusa,Sarajevo 1.05
end

coin scudo
  name scudi
  look Scudi and giuli of the Grand Duke and of the Pope, with saints on one side and their arms on the other.
  in Italy
end

coin spanish
  name Spanish reales
  look Pieces of eight, reales de a ocho, from the silver mountain of Potosí: the coin the whole world takes, and the Levant above all.
  in Spain
  dear Constantinople,Ragusa,Sarajevo,Venice 1.15
end

coin akce
  name akçe
  look Little silver akçe of the Sultan, a great many to the ducat, and more of them to it every year.
  in Ottoman
end

coin ragusan
  name Ragusan coin
  look Grossetti and ducats of the Republic of Saint Blaise, who is on every one of them.
  in Ragusa
end

coin polish
  name Polish złote
  look Groszy, trojaki and the King's thalers, struck at Cracow and at Danzig, and reckoned in złote.
  in Poland
end

coin swedish
  name Swedish dalers
  look Silver dalers and copper öre of the King of Sweden, who has more copper than silver, and pays his soldiers in it.
  in Sweden
end
