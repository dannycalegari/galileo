title What became of them

page Galileo
line if player man-history: On 22 June 1633 Galileo abjured, on his knees, at Santa Maria sopra Minerva, and was not burned, nor kept in a cell. He was sent to the Archbishop of Siena, and in December home to Arcetri, under house arrest for the rest of his life. He went blind in 1637 and 1638, and died at Arcetri on 8 January 1642, aged seventy-seven. This is what happened.
line if player forgery-doubted: Galileo abjured at the Minerva. The forged paper was laid before the Congregation, and enough of them doubted it that he was not condemned as a relapsed heretic; but he was condemned all the same, and never let go home: he lived under guard in Rome, and never saw his daughter again. This is what nearly happened.
line if player man-worse and not player forgery-doubted: Galileo abjured at the Minerva, but too few voices spoke for him, and he was never let go home: he lived under guard in Rome, and never saw his daughter again. This is what nearly happened.
line if player man-worst: Galileo was condemned on a forged paper as a relapsed heretic. The Pope, who had once been his friend, spared him the fire; he died in a cell of the Holy Office in the winter of 1634. This is what nearly happened.
line if player forgery-stopped: The forged injunction, signed "Galileo Galilei" in another man's hand, was burned, and nobody has ever seen it. In the Holy Office's file there is still the unsigned minute of 1616, which historians have argued over since 1870.
line if player arellano-exposed: Don Luis de Arellano was recalled to Madrid that summer, and is not heard of again.

line if player book-history: The copies you put into safe hands were not burned. This is what happened.

This is close to what happened.
