# Things that happen by themselves, whether or not you are there; each happens once.
# The game begins on Tuesday 15 June 1632.
#
#   event ID
#     on YYYY-MM-DD [HH:MM]      not before then (without "on": as soon as the conditions hold)
#     if WHO CONDITION and WHO CONDITION ...
#                                WHO is a person (town:id, as paris:martin) or player; the
#                                condition is as in people's files (has:dialogue, town=London,
#                                stock:dialogue >= 1, not KEY, recent KEY ...)
#     stock WHO ITEM N           a shopkeeper has N more of a thing to sell
#     buy WHO SHOPKEEPER ITEM    WHO buys one (if the shop has one)
#     give FROM TO ITEM          one passes from FROM to TO (if FROM has it)
#     set WHO KEY[=VALUE]        (VALUE "now": the time); unset WHO KEY; add WHO KEY N
#                                WHO may also be TOWN:* (everyone of that town)
#     travel WHO TOWN            WHO sets off for TOWN
#     news TOWN TEXT             told you (status line and journal) if you are in TOWN
#                                ("anywhere": wherever you are)
#     wants TOWN ITEM FACTOR     what TOWN pays for ITEM changes (a siege); FACTOR 1: the plain
#     makes TOWN ITEM FACTOR     price again (market.txt; made again when a saved game is loaded)
#     card ID                    a chapter or date card (data/cards.txt) is shown: in an event with a
#                                jump, after the clock has moved and before the jump's letter or scenes
#     chance P                   when its time comes it happens with this chance, or never; then
#                                you remember failed:ID (another event can wait for that)
#   end
#
# People who remember wants:ITEM buy one themselves when they stand near a shopkeeper
# who has it (People::wantsStep); the events below do it for them when you are not there.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- the Dialogue travels
# Landini printed a thousand copies in Florence in February. Bales of books go north over
# the Alps to the Frankfurt fair, and from there down the Rhine to Holland and across to
# London: so the Dialogue reaches Amsterdam first, then Paul's Churchyard.

event dialogue-reaches-amsterdam
  on 1632-06-21 09:00
  stock amsterdam:blaeu dialogue 2
  set amsterdam:* heard-dialogue-in-town
  news Amsterdam A Rhine barge from Cologne has brought Willem Blaeu a bale of books from the Frankfurt fair: two copies of Galileo's Dialogue among them.
end

# Descartes buys one of Blaeu's copies, if you have not sold him one first
event descartes-buys-from-blaeu
  on 1632-06-22 11:00
  if amsterdam:descartes not has:dialogue
  buy amsterdam:descartes amsterdam:blaeu dialogue
  set amsterdam:descartes from-blaeu
end

event dialogue-reaches-london
  on 1632-06-24 08:00
  stock london:bookseller dialogue 1
  set london:* heard-dialogue-in-town
  news London Midsummer Day: a bale from the Frankfurt fair has come up the river to Paul's Churchyard. They say there is an Italian book in it.
end

# Martin, waiting in London for a copy for Father Mersenne, buys it (if he is there, and
# if he has not bought it in person already, and if it is still there)
event martin-buys-in-london
  on 1632-06-24 13:00
  if paris:martin wants:dialogue and paris:martin town=London and london:bookseller stock:dialogue >= 1
  buy paris:martin london:bookseller dialogue
end

event martin-goes-home
  if paris:martin has:dialogue and paris:martin town=London
  travel paris:martin Paris
  news London Martin Lebrun has gone home to Paris with the only copy of the Dialogue in London, singing.
end

event martin-brings-dialogue
  if paris:martin has:dialogue and paris:martin town=Paris
  give paris:martin paris:mersenne dialogue
  set paris:mersenne has-dialogue
  set paris:mersenne from-martin
  set paris:martin gave-dialogue
  news Paris Martin Lebrun is back from London with a book under his arm, and has run all the way to the Place Royale.
end

# if you got Father Mersenne a copy first, Martin is waiting in London for nothing
event martin-gives-up
  if paris:mersenne has-dialogue and paris:martin wants:dialogue
  unset paris:martin wants:dialogue
  set paris:martin mersenne-has-it
end

event martin-comes-home-empty
  if paris:martin mersenne-has-it and paris:martin town=London and paris:martin not has:dialogue
  travel paris:martin Paris
end

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Pisa's feasts
# The eve of San Ranieri, patron of Pisa (16 June): the Luminara, the palaces along the Arno lit
# with little lamps in glasses; on his day, the regatta of the four quarters. On the last Sunday
# of June (the 27th in 1632), the Gioco del Ponte on the Ponte di Mezzo, Tramontana (the north
# bank) against Mezzogiorno (the south): people with "16:00 go ... if gioco-day" go to watch.

event luminara
  on 1632-06-16 21:00
  set pisa:* luminara
  news Pisa The eve of San Ranieri: every window and cornice along the Arno is lit with little lamps in glasses, thousands upon thousands, and the river doubles them.
end

event regatta-san-ranieri
  on 1632-06-17 16:00
  news Pisa The regatta of San Ranieri: the boats of the four quarters race up the Arno, and a boy of San Martino is first up the mast at the finish to seize the banner.
end

event gioco-del-ponte
  on 1632-06-27 06:00
  set pisa:* gioco-day
  news Pisa The last Sunday of June: today the Gioco del Ponte, Tramontana against Mezzogiorno, on the Ponte di Mezzo. The whole city is going.
end

event gioco-del-ponte-fought
  on 1632-06-27 18:00
  news Pisa On the Ponte di Mezzo, after an hour of shoving in armour behind wooden shields, Mezzogiorno has driven Tramontana off the bridge. The south bank is drinking; the north bank is counting its bruises.
  set pisa:* gioco-won
end

event gioco-del-ponte-over
  on 1632-06-28 03:00
  unset pisa:* gioco-day
end

# The siege of Nuremberg (nuremberg.txt monro and kornmesser; zirndorf.txt), set going by you, so
# that it happens where you are. Historically: Wallenstein's army came on 17
# July 1632, the storming of the Alte Veste was on 3 September, the King marched away on 18
# September. Here: Monro sends you to look for Wallenstein at Zirndorf, and he comes when you get
# there; the hunger ten days later; the astrologer's rumour carried back to Monro, or the truth seen
# in the ruin, brings the King's attack two days
# later; it lasts a day; the King marches away two weeks after it. The prices
# (market.txt: rye 1.8, salt 1.5 before) go with them.
event wallenstein-comes
  if player scouting and player town=Zirndorf and player not event:siege-ends and player not ch2-open
  set player siege-at=now
  wants Nuremberg rye 3.0
  wants Nuremberg salt 2.4
  news Zirndorf From the ridge by the old castle you see it come along the road from the east: dust first, then banners, then an army, regiment after regiment, with carts and guns and women and cattle. By evening the ridge is a town of tents, and the pioneers are filling gabions with earth. Wallenstein has come.
end

event nuremberg-hunger
  if player event:wallenstein-comes and player not recent:10 siege-at and player not event:siege-ends and player not ch2-open
  wants Nuremberg rye 4.5
  wants Nuremberg salt 3.0
  news Nuremberg There is hunger in the city and typhus in both camps. Rye has never been so dear.
end

# The drums of the muster and of the march are the English March, as Charles I's warrant of 7 February 1632 set it down
# (data/music.txt "cue english-march"; music/english_march.py), played on side drums.
# Any of the three reports to Monro sets the storming (nuremberg.txt monro: warned, lied to for nothing, or for the Duke's
# money), and the King goes up two days after, at first light: "the day after tomorrow, at dawn". History holds.
# The muster and the battle, on every road: the evening after the report, at
# eight, the Scots muster in the square at Nuremberg (nuremberg.txt monro, murdo; roles.txt scot); at three they march,
# and if you fell in with Monro you are carried with them to the foot of the ridge under the ruin (the-march); at first
# light the storming, an honest battle (roles.txt scotstorm, assault, imperial, imperialreserve); at ten the Duke's guns
# in the ruin fire, the drums beat the retreat, and the Croats come out to strip the fallen. Murdo Ross is down under
# the wall if you warned Monro, and killed if you lied (the-volley). Beaten on the hill that day, or by the Croats after,
# you wake in the hospital of the Holy Ghost with nothing lost ("carried-down": Game::defeated).
event the-muster
  if player storming-set and player day:1 report-at and player hour>=20 and player not event:alte-veste and player not event:siege-ends and player not ch2-open
  set player muster-on
  news Nuremberg Drums in the streets, beating the muster: the Scots are gathering in the square with their packs and muskets, to march before dawn.
  news Zirndorf Far off, across the valley, drums are beating in Nuremberg.
  cue Nuremberg english-march
  cue Zirndorf english-march 0.25
  wake
end

# at three, if you fell in with Monro (nuremberg.txt monro: "mustered") and are in the city: the march, a card, and you
# stand at the foot of the ridge under the ruin at five, with Murdo Ross beside you (Game::takeRequests); the camera
# turned to look from the north, the battle in front and the town behind it (the march turns the camera to
# 202°; Z turns it back). You are set down outside the ruin's north-west corner, just behind the Scots' line, where
# the Duke's reserve comes down from the north and the fighting is for most of the morning (the ruin stands
# between you and the fighting)
event the-march
  if player muster-on and player mustered and player town=Nuremberg and player day:2+ report-at and player hour>=3 and player hour<5 and player not event:siege-ends and player not ch2-open
  unset player muster-on
  set player marched
  pass until 5:00
  carry 3949 3927 beside nuremberg:murdo
  view 202
  card the-march
  cue anywhere english-march
  wake
end

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- the central plot, chapter I
# Nothing here waits on a date: the plot starts when you take it up, and its deadlines count from
# what you did. Galileo makes you his messenger once he trusts you and has
# had Castelli's warning: carried by you (warned-galileo), or come by the post two days after you
# became his friend (the citron, or the milk and eggs). Fra Clemente's men take the Dialogue at
# Landini's the morning after you collect the bale (so that night is your chance to get it out of
# Florence); if you never collect it, two weeks after the commission; at once, if you tell the
# Inquisitor. Then it is forbidden in Florence, Pisa and Rome, searched for going in and coming
# out (Game::searchers). The keepers are counted as they come to hold a copy, however they got it
# (player keepers); with three, after the seizure, a letter calls you back to Arcetri, and
# Galileo's scene there ends the chapter (ch1-over).

end

event bahuche-dies-waiting
  if player bq-asked and player not recent:30 bq-asked and player not bq-done and player not bahuche-dead
  set player bahuche-dead
  news anywhere A letter finds you, in a boy's careful hand: "Madame Bahuche died in her sleep on Sunday, and they have buried her at Charenton. She left me her colours and her stone, and said you would bring the rest, and I was to do it as she showed me. I am at the gallery every day. Charles Le Brun."
end

  if player bq-done and player not recent:4 bq-done and player not bahuche-dead
  set player bahuche-dead
  news Paris They say in Paris that Marguerite Bahuche, keeper of the King's pictures, has died in her lodging under the Grande Galerie, and has been buried at Charenton, with the Protestants.
end
