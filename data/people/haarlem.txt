# June 1632: Frans Hals is painting the officers of the Saint Hadrian guard (finished next year);

    name: Frans Hals, painter, of Antwerp once, of Haarlem these forty years. {name}
    job: I paint people as they are, quickly, before they stop laughing. This year the officers of the Saint Hadrian guard: twelve of them, and every one of them wants to be in the front.
    debt,bill,lion,money,beer if not player paid-hals: (Lowering his voice.) The landlord of the Golden Lion says I owe him for a year of beer: four livres. Four! If some kind soul paid it for me... I would put him in a painting.
    leyster,judith: Judith Leyster? She paints like a man, and better than most of them. She will be in the Guild next year, mark my words, and then she will take my pupils.
    painting,paint,brush: Do not lick it smooth. A face is never still: paint it moving.

    name: Judith Leyster. {name}
    job: I paint: merry drinkers, children with cats, musicians. People buy them, which is more than can be said for most paintings.
    star,leyster,sign,brewery: My father's brewery is called the Leyster, the lodestar; so I sign my pictures with a star, beside my name.
    guild: The Guild of Saint Luke has never had a woman master. Next year it will.
    hals,frans: Frans Hals paints the way he talks: too fast, and very well. Do not lend him money.
  end

person saenredam
  name Pieter Saenredam
  title a painter of churches
  role painter
  look plain
  near Grote Kerk
  work landmark Grote Kerk
  talk
    name: Pieter Saenredam. I paint churches, and nothing else. {name}
    job: I measure the church with a rod and a line, and draw it true, every pillar and every rib of the vault; then I paint it a little taller than it is, so it looks as tall as it feels when you walk in.
    measure,geometry,perspective,drawing: Perspective is only geometry: a point for the eye, lines to every corner, and a glass between. The difficulty is to make it lie a little, honestly.
    church,bavo,kerk: Saint Bavo: forty years of building, and then they whitewashed it all. I like it white. You can see the light.
  end
end

    name: Barent Cardoes, florist, which is to say a dealer in tulips. {name}
    job: I buy bulbs in the autumn and sell them in the spring; and in between I buy and sell them on paper, many times over, which is better.
    tulip,tulips,bulb,bulbs,clusius if not player gave-tulip: I would give twenty livres for one bulb of old Clusius's own breaking, out of the Hortus at Leiden. His bulbs were stolen from that garden in his own lifetime; that is how all Holland came to have tulips. (He looks at the ceiling.) I do not say steal another. I say twenty livres.
    give:tulip-bulb: (He holds it to the light, turns it, and almost weeps.) From the Hortus! A broken Viceroy, by the look of the skin. Here: twenty livres, and if anyone asks, I bought it from a sailor. {take} {player add money 4800} {player set gave-tulip}
    semper,augustus: Semper Augustus! There are twelve bulbs of it in the world, and one man owns them all, and will not sell. They say he was offered a house on the Herengracht for one.
  end
end

    name: Cornelis Claeszoon, papermaker, of the mill by the Spaarne. {name}
    job: I make paper, the best in Holland, from linen rags and good water. Half of it goes to Amsterdam for the printers, and the rest to Italy, where they think it is their own.
    watermark,=mark,=marks,mould,shield,paper,sheet,wastepaper if player has:practice-sheet: (He holds it to the light and laughs.) That is my father's shield! The crowned shield with the bend, and our initials. He made that mould in the year twenty-two, and I helped him wire it: you can see where the wire was mended, there, in the crown. Paper from that mould did not exist before 1622. Whoever wrote a date of 1616 on it is a liar. {player set proof-paper}
    paper: Good paper is a matter of rags, water and patience. Bad paper is a matter of hurry.
    watermark,=mark,=marks,mould,shield,forgery,forged: Every mill wires its own mark into the mould, and you see it when you hold the sheet up to the light. Bring me a sheet, and I will tell you whose mill it came from, and the year of the mould.
  end
