# The people of Leiden.
# June 1632: Golius teaches Arabic and mathematics; the Elzevirs print their little books for all
# Europe; Rembrandt's mother lives on by the Weddesteeg, two years a widow.
town Leiden
names dutch
prison Town Hall of Leiden | They lock you in the cells under the Town Hall. A student in the next cell, there for fighting the watch, recites Horace through the wall all night. In the morning they let you both go.
country Netherlands

person golius
  name Jacobus Golius
  title a professor of Arabic and mathematics
  role scholar
  near Academy
  work landmark Academy
  talk
    hello if player has:pappus-solution: You have Descartes's answer? Give it to me! {player set knows:amsterdam:descartes}
    name: Jacobus Golius, professor of Arabic, and of mathematics since Snellius died. {name}
    job: I teach Arabic, and the stars, and geometry; I have been to Morocco and to Aleppo, and come back with more manuscripts than I can read.
    pappus,problem,geometry if not player carried-pappus: Pappus of Alexandria left a problem nobody has solved: given four lines, find all the points whose distances to them stand in a given ratio. I sent it to Monsieur Descartes in Amsterdam; he has not answered. Take him this copy, and stand over him until he does. {player add has:pappus 1} {player set carried-pappus} {player set knows:amsterdam:descartes}
    give:pappus-solution: (He reads it, reads it again, and sits down.) He has done it. He gives each line a letter, and the distances become an equation, and the place of the points is a conic. With letters for lines, and x and y... This is not a solution; it is a new geometry. Here: take this, and my thanks, and come to my lectures. {take} {player add money 480} {player learn geometry}
    arabic,manuscripts: Here is Apollonius on conics, in Arabic: three books that are lost in Greek. And here, Ibn al-Haytham on light, which Descartes ought to read before he writes any more on the rainbow. {player set knows:amsterdam:descartes}
  end
end

  near Academy
  work landmark Academy
  talk
    name: Daniel Heinsius, professor of Greek and of politics, poet, and Librarian. {name}
    job: I keep the Library: Scaliger's books, and Lipsius's, and the globes, and the students out of the cupboards.
    galileo: Galileo? A great man for the heavens. In poetry he is a Florentine, which is to say he thinks Ariosto is better than Tasso. He is wrong.
  end

person elzevir
  name Abraham Elzevir
  title a printer of Leiden
  role printer
  near Elzevirs
  work landmark Elzevirs
  sells book 3
  talk
    give:manuscript: (He weighs it in his hands, then opens it, and reads standing for a while.) Galileo's new book. (He shuts it.) It will be in type within the month, in Latin and Tuscan characters, on our best paper. Come back for your copy. {take} {player set manuscript-sent=now} {player set via-leiden}
    book,printed,copy,discorsi,sciences if player science-printed and not player has-printed-copy: (He hands you a book still smelling of the press.) The first one off it. Take it to him. {player add has:two-new-sciences 1} {player set has-printed-copy}
    book,printed,copy,discorsi,sciences if player manuscript-sent and not player science-printed: In the press now. Come back in a few days.
    give:dialogue if has:dialogue >= 1: We have one, in the back. One is enough to print from.
    give:dialogue: (He turns it over: the paper, the type, the woodcut, like a man buying a horse.) Landini prints well, for an Italian. I will keep it in the shop's own library, not for sale. If Rome bans it, as you say, then one day somebody will want it printed again, a long way from Rome; and when that day comes the Elzevirs will have the copy. {take} {player set knows:florence:landini}
    name: Abraham Elzevir, printer to the University. {name}
    job: We print small books, in Latin, cheaply and well: the Republics of every country in the world, one to a little volume, that a man can carry in his coat. All Europe buys them.
    galileo,dialogue: If Signor Galileo ever needs a printer a long way from Rome, tell him the Elzevirs print what is true, and what sells, and do not ask too many questions about which is which.
  end
end

person heurnius
  name Otto Heurnius
  title the keeper of the anatomy theatre
  role physician
  near anatomy theatre
  work landmark anatomy theatre
  talk
    name: Otto Heurnius, professor of anatomy, and keeper of the theatre and its curiosities. {name}
    job: In winter, when the bodies keep, we dissect, here in the theatre, and the town comes to watch. In summer I show the curiosities, for two sous.
    give:money if player gift >= 24 and not player saw-theatre: (He leads you round the tiers.) Skeletons, as you see, of men and beasts, holding banners: "Pulvis et umbra sumus", we are dust and shadow; a skeleton riding a skeleton horse; an Egyptian mummy, a crocodile, a Laplander's sledge, a Chinese paper, and the skin of a thief, tanned. A lesson to us all. {take} {player set saw-theatre}
    give:money: Thank you. The dead thank you too. {take}
    thief,skin: A thief, hanged, and dissected, and his skin tanned like leather. You did not touch anything in the town, I hope?
  end
end

person gardener
  name Dirck Outgersz
  title the gardener of the Hortus
  role farmer
  near Ambulacrum
  work landmark Ambulacrum
  talk
    name: Dirck Outgersz, gardener of the Hortus of the University. {name}
    job: I keep the beds: a thousand plants in squares, each with its name, for the students of medicine. And the bulbs.
    tulip,tulips,bulb,bulbs,clusius: Old Clusius brought the first tulips here from Vienna, forty years ago, and would not sell them for any price; so they were stolen, by night, out of this very garden, and now every merchant in Holland grows them. We keep the best in the Ambulacrum now, and I sleep with one eye open.
    hortus,garden,plants: Potatoes from Peru, the tomato that is a poison, the passion flower that shows the nails of the Cross; and a dragon tree, which bleeds red.
  end
end

    name: Neeltgen Willemsdochter van Zuytbrouck, the widow of the miller Harmen. {name}
    job: I was a miller's wife; the mill is on the wall there, the Rijn mill, my eldest son works it. Now I keep house, and pray for my children.
    son,sons,rembrandt,letter if not player carried-mother-letter: My Rembrandt has gone to Amsterdam, to be rich and famous, and he never writes. If you go there, take him this letter, and tell him to eat. {player add has:mother-letter 1} {player set carried-mother-letter}
    son,sons,rembrandt,letter: He painted me reading my Bible, last year, with my nose in it. He made me look a hundred. I am sixty-four.
  end
end
