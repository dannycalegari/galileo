# The people of Padua.
# June 1632: Padua is Venice's, and its University (the Bo) is the Republic's pride: here a
# Protestant may study, and here Galileo taught for eighteen years, until 1610. The plague of
# 1631 has just passed; Cremonini, who would not look through the spyglass, died of it.
town Padua
names venetian
prison Castello | The Venetians keep the old Castello of the Carrara as their prison. You spend the night at the top of the Torlonga, Ezzelino's tower, with a bandit from the Euganean hills who swears he is innocent and snores like a bellows. The warder says the tower will hold nothing worse than astronomers one day. In the morning they let you go.

    name: Fortunio Liceti, of Rapallo, Professor of Philosophy in the University of Padua. {name}
    job: I teach Aristotle, as the statutes require, and I write books: on monsters, on the soul of plants, on why the old lamps of the pagans burned for ever. Signor Galileo and I have quarrelled by letter for twenty years, and remain friends.
    galileo: My friend and my adversary. He says the heavens are made of the same stuff as the Earth; I say Aristotle saw further without a spyglass than Galileo with one. We write each other long letters, and neither of us gives an inch.
    earthshine,moon,light if player heard-earthshine and not player has-stone-from-liceti: (He frowns.) The Earth lights the Moon? That is what he says, is it? I say the Moon has a little light of her own, as a certain stone has. Here: take this, from my cabinet, and look at it in the dark. Then tell me which of us is mad. {player add has:bologna-stone 1} {player set has-stone-from-liceti}
    earthshine,moon,light: The Moon, when she is new, shows a faint grey light over all her dark part. Where does it come from? Not from the Sun. I think she has a little light of her own, and drinks the Sun's as a certain *stone* does.
    stone,bologna,lapis if player has-stone-from-liceti: Let it lie in the sun by day, and it will glow for you at night. A cobbler of Bologna found it, trying to make gold. He made something better.
    stone,bologna,lapis: The stone of Bologna. You roast it, and then it drinks the daylight and gives it back in the dark, like the Moon. If I could show you one... but they are rare. Ask me about the *moon*.
    cremonini: Cesare Cremonini, my colleague, the greatest Aristotelian in Italy. He would not look through Galileo's spyglass: it gave him a headache, he said, and besides, what could it show him that Aristotle had not? The plague took him last summer. He sees the heavens now, if anyone does.
    monster,monsters: I wrote a book on them: a child with two heads, a lamb with a man's face. Nature errs, and in her errors you see how she works when she does not.
    dialogue,book: The new Dialogue? I have read it twice, once in anger and once in admiration. My poor Simplicio! He is a better philosopher than Galileo allows him to be.
  end
end

    name: Johann Vesling, of Minden in Westphalia, Professor of Anatomy and Surgery. They gave me the chair this year. {name}
    job: I open the human body before four hundred students, in the theatre Fabricius built. In winter, that is: in June the dead will not keep.
    theatre,anatomy,dissection: Six rings of standing room round the table, steep as a well, and the students lean on the rails and look down. Some faint; we catch them. There is a little door to the canal beneath, by which the bodies come in at night, by boat. Nobody asks from where.
    fabricius: Girolamo Fabrici of Acquapendente, my great predecessor. He found the little doors in the veins, the valves, and never understood what they were for. An Englishman did, his pupil.
    harvey,heart,blood if player has:de-motu-cordis: (He takes the book and turns the pages, fast, then slowly.) Harvey! A son of this house, a doctor of Padua of 1602. The blood goes round, out by the arteries and back by the veins, and Fabricius's valves are its doors... It is so simple I am angry I did not think of it. Keep it safe: half the physicians of Europe would burn it. {player set showed-vesling}
    harvey,heart,blood: William Harvey, the Englishman: he studied here under Fabricius. He has written a little book that says the blood goes round the body, always the same blood. If you ever see it, bring it to me.
    egypt,cairo,coffee,kahve: I was in Egypt four years ago, looking for plants for the Orto. In Cairo every street has a house where men drink kahve, a black water, and talk politics all night. Prospero Alpini wrote of it first. Ask his son, at the *Orto*.
    orto,garden,botanic: The Orto botanico, behind the Santo: the garden of simples, the oldest in the world. Alpino Alpini keeps it, as his father did.
  end

    name: Alpino Alpini, prefect of the Orto botanico, as my father was before me. {name}
    job: I keep the garden of simples: the plants a physician must know, and the strange ones the Republic's ships bring home. Everything has its bed, and its label, and its season.
    alpini,prospero,father: Prospero Alpini, my father. He went to Cairo as physician to the Venetian consul and came back with three hundred plants and a book. He was the first Christian to describe the coffee tree: *kahve*, the Turks say.
    coffee,kahve,beans if has-coffee: My beans are in the ground, under glass. Every morning I look; every morning nothing. Patience.
    coffee,kahve,beans: The Turks drink it roasted and ground in hot water, black, and say it keeps them awake through the night prayers. I would give a great deal for a handful of green beans, unparched, to sow. The Turkish merchants of Venice may have some, at the Fondaco.
    palm: That palm by the wall was planted in 1585: the year I was born. It will outlive us all. One day, I tell my gardeners, a German poet will come and stand under it, and write a book about the shapes of leaves.
    orto,garden,oldest,pisa: The oldest garden of its kind in the world, founded by the Senate in 1545. Pisa says hers is older. Pisa's has moved house twice; ours is where it was planted. Ask them in Pisa, and watch them go red.

    name: Polo Rizzo, beadle of the University of the Artists, at your service. {name}
    job: I carry the mace before the Rector, ring the bell, fine the students, and show strangers the chair of Signor Galileo, for a small consideration.
    chair,cattedra,lectern if player paid-bidello: There it is, in the hall: that wooden stand on its steps, like a pulpit for a carpenter. The students knocked it together themselves so that more of them could see him, he drew such crowds. He stood up there and showed them the new star of 1604, and the hall was full to the windows.
    chair,cattedra,lectern,galileo: Signor Galileo's chair? Eighteen years he taught here, the best years, he says. For a sou I shall show it to you.
    give:money if player gift >= 12: (He pockets it, and bows.) This way. Mind the step. {take} {player set paid-bidello}
    bo,ox,name: The Bo is the ox: this was the inn of the Ox before the University bought it, two hundred years ago. The students say it still smells of the stable at examinations.
    woman,women,degree: A woman? A doctor of Padua? Never, while the Bo stands. (He thinks.) Though the Senate has done stranger things.
    copernicus: A Pole, Koppernigk, studied medicine here, a hundred and thirty years ago, and went home and set the Earth going round the Sun. We do not mention him at examinations.
  end
