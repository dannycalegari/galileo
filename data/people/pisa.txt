# The people of Pisa.
# June 1632: Pisa is the Grand Duke's, and its University (the Sapienza) is where Galileo
# studied and first taught. His pupil Niccolò Aggiunti holds the chair of mathematics; Scipione
# Chiaramonti, whom the new Dialogue mocks at length, the chair of philosophy. The eve of San
# Ranieri (16 June) is the Luminara, the last Sunday of June (the 27th) the Gioco del Ponte.
town Pisa
names italian
prison Cittadella Vecchia | They lock you in the Guelph Tower of the old citadel, over the river. All night the warder tells you the story of Count Ugolino, starved to death in a tower of this city with his sons and grandsons, and what Dante says he did then. At breakfast you are not hungry. In the morning they let you go.

    job: I teach Euclid to young men who would rather be fencing, and I write on why water climbs up a narrow straw by itself, which nobody can explain, not even my master.

    lamp,pendulum,duomo: The lamp in the Duomo? The story is that he timed its swinging against his pulse, at Mass, and found that wide swings and small take the same time. The sacristan will tell you more, and less.
    chiaramonti: My colleague of the chair of philosophy. My master's Dialogue spends half a day proving that his calculations of the new star of 1572 are nonsense. He has not read it yet. I am keeping out of his way when he does.
    water,straw,capillary: Dip a fine glass straw in water and the water rises in it, higher the finer the straw. Against its weight! There is no vacuum to pull it. I have a theory. My master says it is wrong.

    tower,balls,drop,leaning: The fable of the tower? A heavy body falls faster than a light one, in proportion to its weight: Aristotle, On the Heavens, the first book.     stars,star,nova,parallax: A new star in the heavens is impossible: the heavens do not change. So the new stars were not in the heavens. That is logic, sir.

    tomato,tomatoes,golden: Golden apples from the Indies. The Grand Duke's steward had a basket of them, eighty years ago, the first in Italy. We grow them for their looks. Some say they are poisonous; some fry them. I have done both; I am still alive.

person sacristan
  name Ser Piero Neri
  title the sacristan of the Duomo
  role priest
  near Duomo of Pisa
  work landmark Duomo of Pisa
  talk
    name: Piero Neri, sacristan of the Primaziale. {name}
    job: I light the lamps, ring the bells, sweep out the pigeons, and answer the same question forty times a day.
    lamp,pendulum,galileo: (He sighs.) Yes, that is the lamp. Bronze, by Battista Lorenzi. Young Galileo watched it swing at Mass, they say, and timed it with his pulse. It was hung in 1587. He went away to Florence in 1585. The lamp he watched is in the Camposanto, in a chapel. Nobody wants to see that one.
    pulpit,pisano: Giovanni Pisano's pulpit, of 1310. After the fire of 1595 they took it to pieces and put it in boxes. We are still arguing about where the pieces go. The Baptistery's, by his father Nicola, is whole.
    tower,leaning: The campanile began to lean when they had built the third storey, four hundred years ago. They waited a hundred years, built the rest leaning the other way, and it leaned more. It is the only thing in Pisa that is not in a hurry.
    fire: In 1595 the roof burned, and the bronze doors melted, and half the treasures. The Grand Duke paid for a new roof, with his arms on it.
  end
end

    name: Lisabetta. I was born a Gori, and I married a Gori, so nobody has ever had to learn my name. {name}
    job: I am eighty, and I sit at my door. That is a job, at eighty.
    galileo,born,baby,house: He was born in this house, the Ammannati house, sixty-eight years ago, in February. I was a girl of twelve. His mother Giulia had a tongue like a razor, and his father Vincenzio played the lute all day and argued with everyone about music. Three days later Michelangelo died in Rome. My mother said the one soul had gone into the other.
    vincenzio,father,music,lute: Vincenzio Galilei, a Florentine of good family and no money, a lutenist. He wrote a book to prove that the old masters of music had their numbers wrong. The son is the same: he proves people wrong for a living.
  end
end

  near Palazzo della Sapienza
  work landmark Palazzo della Sapienza
  talk
    name: Jacopo Pucci, beadle of the Studio. {name}
    job: I ring the bell, I carry the mace, and I fine the professors who will not wear their gowns.
    gown,gowns,toga,fine,fines: There was a young lecturer of mathematics here, forty years ago, who would not wear his gown in the street. Fined every week! He wrote a poem against gowns, three hundred lines: that a man should go naked, as God made him, and then you would see what he was worth. His name was Galileo. They still talk of the poem. Nobody can recite it in front of the Rector.
    galileo,student: He came here as a student of medicine, in 1581, and left without a degree, for want of money. Then he came back as a professor. Then he left again, for Padua, for want of money. The Studio does not forget.
  end
end

    gioco,ponte,bridge: The Gioco! The last Sunday of June, on the Ponte di Mezzo: six hundred men in armour, with wooden shields, pushing. Tramontana and Mezzogiorno. I am Mezzogiorno. We won last year. We will win this year.
