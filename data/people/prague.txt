# The people of Prague.
# June 1632: the Saxons took Prague last November, with the Protestant exiles behind them; last
# month Wallenstein, the Emperor's generalissimo again, took it back, and the exiles ran again.
# Kepler and Tycho both served the Emperor Rudolf here; Tycho lies in the Týn church. Georg
# Baresch owns a book nobody can read.
town Prague
names czech
prison Old Town hall | They lock you in the cellars of the Old Town hall. Eleven years ago this month, on the square above, they cut off the heads of twenty-seven lords of the Protestant rising, and twelve of the heads hung on the bridge tower for ten years, until the Saxons came last winter and buried them. In the morning they let you go.

    job: I teach medicine at the Carolinum, I physic half the Old Town, and in the evenings I knock ivory balls together on a table, which my colleagues think is beneath a physician.
    balls,ivory,motion,collision,impact: When one ivory ball strikes another as large at rest, the first stops dead and the second goes on with all its motion. Strike two with one, and they share it. There is a proportion in it, and I mean to find it, and write it down.
    galileo,falling,fall: Galileo has a new book, they say. I have not seen it. I am working on falling bodies myself, and I would rather not know yet whether he has beaten me.
    book,cipher,baresch,manuscript: My friend Georg Baresch has a book that nobody can read. Go and see him, if you know any strange languages. He will show it to anybody, and then he will sigh.

person baresch
  name Georg Baresch
  title an alchemist
  role scholar
  look coat #3A2A3A legs #2A222A hat hat #1E1A1E plain
  near Týn
  talk
    name: Georg Baresch. I study the art of chemistry, and other arts. {name}
    job: I have spent twenty years on one book. It is not a book I wrote. It is a book I cannot read.
    book,cipher,manuscript,read if player skill:arabic: You read the Arabic letters? Then look. (You look for a long time. It is not Arabic. It is not Hebrew, or Greek, or anything at all.) No. It is never anything at all.
    book,cipher,manuscript,read: (He unlocks a chest and takes out a little book of vellum: plants that grow nowhere, stars and circles, little naked women bathing in green pools, and on every page a writing in a hand that nobody knows.) It came out of the Emperor Rudolf's cabinet, they say; he paid six hundred ducats for it. I think it is medicine, from the East. If only I could find someone who reads the old languages of Egypt.
    kircher,egypt,hieroglyphs,jesuit: There is a young Jesuit of Würzburg, Father Kircher, who says he can read the hieroglyphs of Egypt. The Swedes chased him out of Würzburg last autumn, and nobody knows where he has gone. If I find him, I shall write to him. He will be able to read it. I am sure of it.
  end
end

person arriaga
  name Father Rodrigo de Arriaga
  title a Jesuit of the Clementinum
  role jesuit
  look plain
  near Clementinum
  work landmark Clementinum
  talk
    name: Rodrigo de Arriaga, of the Society of Jesus, from Logroño in Spain, professor of philosophy at the Clementinum. {name}
    job: I teach philosophy and theology to the young men of Bohemia, and this year my Course of Philosophy is printed at Antwerp: the whole of it, in one volume, and I disagree with everybody in it, Aristotle included, very politely.
    heavens,fluid,spheres,planets: The heavens are not crystal spheres: they are fluid, as thin as air, and the planets move through them like fish in the sea. Tycho proved it with his comets, here in Prague. I say so in my book, and nobody has burned it yet.
    saxons,heretics,exile: When the Saxons came in November the Society had to leave Prague, all of us, on foot. We came back last month behind the Duke of Friedland's army. The library was still here. God is good.
    galileo,earth,moves: Whether the Earth moves is a question for the theologians, and the theologians have answered it. I am only a philosopher. I keep my opinion in a drawer.
  end
end

  talk
    name: Giovanni Battista Senno, of Genoa, mathematician to His Highness the Duke of Friedland. {name}
    job: I watch the sky for the Duke, and cast his nativity again every year, and every year I tell him a little less than I see.
    wallenstein,duke,friedland: His Highness the Duke of Friedland, the Emperor's generalissimo. He took Prague back from the Saxons last month, and now he is gone west with his army to find the King of Sweden. He built this palace to show the Emperor that a subject could be greater than a king. The Emperor noticed.
    kepler,horoscope,nativity: Kepler cast the Duke's nativity, when he was young, and again seven years ago. He said the Duke would be restless, cruel, contemptuous of men, and great. And then for the year 1634 he broke off, and said the rest was superstition. I have looked at 1634 myself. I do not like it.
  end
end

person sexton
  name Matěj Novák
  title the sexton of the Týn church
  role priest
  near Týn
  work landmark Týn
  talk
    name: Matěj Novák, sexton of Our Lady before Týn. {name}
    job: I ring the bells, I sweep, and I show the strangers the tomb of Tycho, who all come for that and not for Our Lady.
    tycho,tomb,brahe,nose: Tycho Brahe, the Emperor Rudolf's astronomer, the Dane. He lies under the stone by the pillar, carved in his armour, with his nose. He lost the real one in a duel over a question of mathematics, and wore one of brass. They say he died of politeness: he would not leave the Emperor's table to make water.
    chalice,hussite,madonna: Over the front of the church there was a great gilded chalice, for the Hussites, who take the communion in both kinds. After the White Mountain the Jesuits took it down and put up the Virgin, and they melted the chalice to make her halo. Do not say that to the Jesuits.
  end
end

    name: Mordechai ben Yehuda, shammes of the Old-New synagogue. {name}
    job: I open the doors and close them, I wake the town for prayer, and I keep the key of the attic, which nobody needs, because nobody goes up there.
    maharal,rabbi,loew,golem,attic: The great Rabbi Loew prayed here, the Maharal, of blessed memory; he died twenty-three years ago and lies in the old cemetery, under a stone with a lion on it. The children say that he made a man of clay to guard the town, and that it lies in the attic, and that is why the stairs stop halfway. Children say a great many things.
    cemetery,stones,graves: The living cannot leave the Jewish town, and neither can the dead. There is no room, so we lay them one above another, twelve deep, and the stones stand crowded together like people at a market.
  end

    clock,orloj,astronomical,hanus: The clock shows the Sun and the Moon going round the Earth through the twelve signs, the hours of old Bohemia counted from sunset, and the hours of the Germans. Master Hanuš made it, they say, and the councillors put out his eyes so he could make no other, and he climbed up into the works and stopped it for a hundred years. It is not true.
