# The people of Ragusa.
# June 1632: the Republic of Ragusa pays the Sultan a tribute every year and trades with
# everybody; its ships are on every sea. Its Rector serves for one month only. Ivan Gundulić,

prison Rector's palace | They lock you in the cells under the Rector's palace. A clerk reads out the law of the Republic to you, at length, in three languages. In the morning they let you go, and a guard walks you to the Pile gate to make sure you have understood.

    name: Luka Sorgo, Rector of the Republic of Ragusa, for the month of June. {name}
    job: I am the Rector for one month. I may not leave the palace without the Senate's leave, I may not receive anybody alone, and on the first of July I go home and somebody else is Rector. That is how a republic keeps its liberty: nobody holds the keys long enough to copy them.
    sultan,tribute,turks: Twelve thousand ducats a year to the Sultan, and he leaves us alone, and our ships fly his flag in his seas and our own everywhere else. The Venetians say we are the Turk's lap dog. The Venetians have never forgiven us for being rich without them.
    liberty,freedom,lovrijenac: Over the gate of Lovrijenac it is written: "Non bene pro toto libertas venditur auro". Liberty is not sold for all the gold in the world. We sell everything else.

    name: Ivan Gundulić, senator, and in the evenings a poet, which is a worse job and better paid in fame. {name}
    job: I am writing an epic of the war of the Poles against Sultan Osman, eleven years ago, in our own tongue: twenty cantos. If it is not finished when I die, my sons will have to do it.
    poem,osman,epic: Osman, the young Sultan, who lost his war at Khotyn and was strangled by his own janissaries. Fortune's wheel: the higher you go, the further you fall. A good subject for a republic.
    liberty,freedom,dubravka: "O lijepa, o draga, o slatka slobodo": O beautiful, O dear, O sweet liberty. I wrote it for a play four years ago, and now the fishermen sing it. That is the only fame worth having.
  end
end

    job: I own a part of eleven ships, a third of this one, a quarter of that, so that no storm can ruin me. They carry salt from Ston, wool from the Balkans and grain from Apulia, to Venice, Alexandria and Cadiz.
    ships,argosy,argosies: The English call our great ships argosies, after Ragusa. One of their poets wrote a play about a merchant of Venice, whose argosies were all lost at sea. A Venetian, of course. Ours come home.
    salt,ston: The salt of Ston is the Republic's, and its price is the Republic's too. Buy it here; inland the Turks pay three times as much.

    job: Every ship and every caravan from a place where there has been plague waits here forty days, men and goods, before it comes into the town. We were the first to do it, two hundred and fifty years ago. Venice copied us, and tells everyone it was her idea.
    quarantine,plague,forty: Forty days: quaranta giorni. We began with thirty, the trentino, in 1377; the Venetians made it forty and gave the thing its name. The plague does not care what it is called.
