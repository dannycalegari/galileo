# The people of Venice.
# June 1632: the plague ended last November, after taking a third of the city; the church of

    spyglass,telescope if player has:spyglass: A spyglass? Show it me. If you mean to part with it, the Republic will make it worth your while.

    name: Francesco Erizzo, by the grace of God Doge of Venice. {name}
    job: The Doge reigns, and the Senate governs. I preside, I sign, I wed the sea on Ascension Day. And I am watched, as every Doge is watched, lest he forget he is a servant.
    sea,wedding,ascension: Every Ascension Day we row out in the Bucintoro to the Lido, and I throw a gold ring into the waves: "We wed thee, O sea, in sign of true and perpetual dominion."
    plague: It took a third of us: forty-six thousand souls in the city. Doge Contarini died in the worst of it, and they made me Doge. We have vowed a church to the Virgin of Health, at the mouth of the Grand Canal. Longhena is building it.
    salute,longhena: Baldassare Longhena, a young man. Eight sides, and a great dome. It will take him the rest of his life.
    galileo: He taught at Padua for eighteen years, in our pay. We doubled his salary for the spyglass, and he left us for Florence. Venice does not forget, and does not hold a grudge; we have use for both.
    pope,rome: Rome is welcome to its opinions. We had an Interdict on us twenty-six years ago, and Fra Paolo told the Pope that Venice was a prince in its own house. We are still here.
    sarpi,paolo: Fra Paolo Sarpi, our theologian. They tried to murder him in the street, with stilettos; he said he knew the style of the Roman Curia. He died nine years ago. Fra Fulgenzio has his place.
  end
end

    print,printers,books: Half the books of Europe were printed in Venice, once. If Galileo wants a book printed that Rome will not license, there are printers in Holland now who ask fewer questions.

    name: Don Claudio Monteverdi, of Cremona, master of music at San Marco these nineteen years. {name}
    job: I direct the music of the Basilica: two choirs, one in each gallery, and the instruments between, so that the sound comes at you from both sides like weather.
    priest,orders: I was ordained this spring, at sixty-five. My wife has been dead these twenty-five years, and the plague taught me to think about my soul.
    plague: We sang a Mass of thanksgiving when it ended, in November, here in San Marco, with trumpets. I wrote a Gloria for it, with seven voices and two violins.
    orfeo,opera: Orfeo, at Mantua, twenty-five years ago. A fable in music: the whole story sung, as the ancients did. They say it is the first of its kind. Arianna made the ladies weep; all of it is lost now but her lament.
    music,piece: This year I have printed a book of little pieces, Scherzi musicali, in the new style: the words first, and the music their servant.
    galileo: His father, Vincenzio, wrote against the old counterpoint and for the singing of a single voice. A clever man. The son is cleverer, but less musical, I am told.
  end
end

    name: Leon Modena, rabbi of the Ghetto, and preacher, and cantor, and teacher of anyone who will pay. {name}
    job: I preach in the synagogue on the Sabbath, and Christian gentlemen come to hear me, and priests, and once a brother of the King of France. I have had twenty-six trades, and all of them poor ones.
    ghetto: The Ghetto Nuovo: an island with two gates, locked at sunset and opened at dawn, and Christian guards whom we must pay ourselves. The houses go up and up, seven storeys, because they cannot go out.
    hat,red: The Republic says we must wear a red hat, or a yellow one, so that nobody can mistake us for gentlemen.
    plague: It came into the Ghetto too. We lost many. God does not ask which gate a man lives behind.
    book,rites: I wrote a book of our rites and customs, for the English ambassador, Sir Henry Wotton, so that Christians might see what we do and not what they imagine. It is not yet printed.
    galileo: The Florentine says the Earth moves? Our sages said many things too, and the Earth went on doing as it pleased. But I have looked through a spyglass, and the Moon has mountains.
    cards,gambling: (He winces.) You have heard about the cards. I wrote a dialogue against gambling when I was thirteen, and I have gambled ever since. Both are true.
  end
end
